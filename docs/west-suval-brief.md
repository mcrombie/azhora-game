# Brief: build West Suval and Solis, and move the parley there

Start from the scaled world (`docs/world-scale-report.md`; one authored hex is 100 m and hand-placed coordinates go through `src/world-scale.js`). West Suval becomes the fifth playable region and Solis the place the main quest goes, so the fork of the story no longer happens on the border of the Moros Plain.

## The region

- Atlas facts: West Suval has 24 hexes (11 grassland, 8 plains, 5 hills), lies east-south-east of the Moros Plain, west of East Suval and south of Luscia, with the sea to its south. The campaign registry (`src/campaign-world.js`) has it at level 2, held by the Coalition, "grassland and low hills toward the coast; Solis on the southwest", threats bandits and wolves. Solis is "the market city of the southwest, first held by the Coalition landing".
- Add `'West Suval'` to `PLAYABLE_REGIONS` with id 5 and a biome of its own in `REGION_BIOMES` (rolling coastal grassland: long grass, scattered thorn and olive-like trees, low stone walls, hills rising toward the coast; clearly different from the flat treeless Moros and from East Suval's grey stone). Regenerate `src/region-survey.js` with `node scripts/build-region-survey.mjs` so the survey covers it and its neighbours. Region membership, outlines, minimap, trail charts, the chart marker, the region card, `WORLD_BOUNDS`, the developer atlas's local destinations and `tests/region-layout.test.js` expectations all follow.
- Roads: a road from the border stockade on the Moros Plain south-east into West Suval to Solis, as a world path with the same conventions as `SUVAL_ROAD`; a branch from East Suval's border post if the outlines make that natural. A signpost where it crosses the border.
- Wilderness between the border and Solis: two or three small places (a shepherds' fold, a ruined watchtower, a wayside well) as landmarks with discovery text, so the region reads as country and not a corridor. Wolves and bandits are scenery or rumours only unless time allows one encounter.

## Fortifications: one standard for both sides

The user wants Solis to be a walled town whose fortifications are about as defensible as the Ambroni Empire's fortified position on the Moros Plain (the Legion camp and its forward stockade on the border). Neither side should look like it could walk over the other. Both briefs carry this section; build to the same measures so the two read as a match when the player has seen both:

- **A continuous circuit** with no gaps a person can walk through except the gates; colliders all the way round, and `canStand` false inside the wall's thickness. Wall top about 4.5 to 5 m above the ground outside, thick enough to carry a wall walk.
- **A wall walk with a parapet** on the inside of the circuit, reached by stairs or ladders at two or more points, and drawn so it reads as a fighting platform from the follow camera.
- **Towers**: two flanking each gate, one at each corner or change of direction, all a storey taller than the wall and projecting slightly so they cover its face. Roughly one tower per 35 to 45 m of wall.
- **Two gates, no more**: a main gate on the road and a lesser one (Solis: a sea or quay gate; the camp: a rear gate). Each gate is a passage through a gatehouse or between its towers with leaves drawn open, 4 to 5 m wide, and guarded.
- **An outer obstacle**: a ditch 3 to 4 m wide all round except at the gate causeways (the terrain can stay flat if the ditch is modelled as geometry with a collider; do not break the road), with the spoil as a low bank under the wall.
- **Materials differ, strength does not**: Solis is dressed stone with tiled tower caps and banners of the Coalition's contingents; the Legion's works are squared timber palisade on an earth rampart with timber towers and the Legate's standard. Same heights, same tower spacing, same ditch.

Solis is the stone half of this. Its circuit encloses the whole town (the Coalition's camp lies outside it, inside its own light picket line), the main gate faces the road from the border, and the lesser gate opens on the quay. Add a test that the circuit is closed: sample the wall line every 2 m and assert nobody can stand on it except in the two gate passages, and that each gate passage can be walked end to end.

## Solis

- **Its history, from the user**: Solis was once the capital of an independent kingdom that ruled West Suval. The Ambroni Empire conquered it, and the Coalition took it from the Empire shortly before the game begins. Let all three layers show: the old kingdom in the bones of the place (the walls, the royal terraces, the Court of Oaths, the sun-horse emblem), the Empire in what it added and imposed (a Legion barracks and tax house by the gate, imperial plaques and milestones, now defaced or boarded), and the Coalition in what is days old (fresh banners of the contingents, a paymaster's table in the old tax house, the camp outside the walls, notices in three hands). Townsfolk remember being a kingdom, resent the Empire and are not yet sure of the Republic.
- **Details from the lore.** `../world-builder/azhora_lore/history/war_of_seven_sons_novella.md` describes Solis. The user's rule: use the novella's details of places and people only where they overlap with places and people already on the game's map, never its events or plot, and do not force its story into the game. For Solis that means: white cliffs above the harbour; terraces of red tile and sun-washed stone climbing from the sea; bronze statues and hanging gardens; old sea walls repaired so many times that ancient and new cannot be told apart; harbour towers with a boom; courtyards of orange trees in the upper town; temple roofs and a temple guest house; the **Court of Oaths** (make it the hall where the Coalition's council sits); and the landward main gate, the **Gate of Sun Horses**, under two rearing bronze horses whose manes are hammered into rays (children throw flowers at them at the summer festival). The sun-horse is the city's emblem. Do not name the novella's characters or refer to its wedding, siege or war.
- A walled market town on the south-west of the region near the coast, clearly the largest settlement in the game so far: the fortifications above with the Gate of Sun Horses as the main gate on the border road and a quay gate through the sea wall, a market square, 18 to 25 buildings of mixed size (houses, warehouses by a quay or beach landing, an inn, a counting house, a small temple or hall where the Coalition's council sits), streets as paths, a well or fountain, stalls. Put its layout in a town frame like Lumber Town's (`townPoint`), in a new module so the existing scenery file does not balloon. Colliders and `canStand` must be right; every NPC stand reachable.
- The Coalition's camp outside the walls: tent lines grouped by contingent with a banner each: Izoli, Suvali, the Ambroni rebels, a very small Pyrosi group (their empire is busy at home), Selemi, Marosh, and the southern island city-states. There is no South Pyros.
- People (cloth roles for townsfolk, `suvali-guard` for Coalition soldiers; soldiers are men by default): gate guards who admit an envoy under the Legate's seal, Envoy Telis Orren (move her here from `src/border-chapter.js`), one captain per major contingent with two lines each that show who they are and why they came, a Solis merchant who grumbles about paper scrip, and four to six townsfolk with ambient lines. Keep the tone rules in `docs/content-pass.md`.

## The quest

- `src/border-chapter.js`: the Legate's terms are carried **to Solis**. Stages become: take the terms from the Legate; pass the Gate of Sun Horses; meet the envoy in the Court of Oaths and choose a side (same two choices, same campaign call).
- **The Coalition's offer is better than the Empire's pay**, as the brief says: the envoy offers plainly more money (the Legate's muster paid 25 copper; she puts 50 copper on the table at once for a sellsword who signs, double the Legate's rate after, and land when the Republic wins). Choosing the Republic pays the 50 copper then and there; keeping the Empire's contract pays nothing extra. Copper only: paper scrip stays talk (the Solis merchant's grumble), not an item.
- **The battle begins with a report and a march**, as the brief says. Empire: ride back to the Legion's outpost on the Moros and report the envoy's answer to the Legate; he asks whether you are ready ("Yes" / "Give me a moment"); on yes the army marches, and the traveler goes with it: the mustered men of the hired company and a file of legionaries walk the road to the border with the traveler (reuse the garrison escort pattern in `src/main.js` and `src/forest-hideout.js`: followers keep pace, catch up out of sight when left far behind, are hidden while they fight as combat allies, and the march survives a save), where Tribune Gallus Orso has the line and the fight starts when the column arrives. Coalition: Captain Arlen Voss asks the same question at the Gate of Sun Horses and the valley companies march with the traveler up the road to the border. The battle is fought at the border as the campaign text says, the Legion on the Moros side of the stockade and the Coalition on the West Suval side. Keep the encounter, the allies, the odds and the save format; add only what the new stages need, and keep saves from the stockade version loading (a save already past the envoy keeps its side).
- The autopilot (`borderGoal`) walks to Solis, keeps the Empire's contract, walks back and fights. `src/autoplay-smoke.js` expectations follow.
- The mercenary company does not go to Solis.

## After the battle, and who holds what

- `src/aftermath-chapter.js` with `src/aftermath-sites.js` is the chapter after the battle, written by the lead and wired into `src/main.js`, the checkpoint and the autopilot. The Empire's sellsword clears the market square of Solis (`solis-sweep`) or holds the Legion camp's gate (`moros-fallback`); the Republic's storms **the Legion's outpost at the centre of the Moros Plain** (`moros-outpost`; the user confirmed that "the outpost" is the Legion camp, and the lead is re-siting that variant there, so do not touch the Moros entries) or holds the gate of Solis (`solis-fallback`). The Solis entries in `src/aftermath-sites.js` are `null`; fill them in:
  - Sites `solis-gate` (outside the Gate of Sun Horses, beside the road, where a commander waits) and `solis-hall` (in or on the steps of the Court of Oaths, where the envoy or the Tribune receives a report): standable, reachable, with a `yaw` facing whoever approaches.
  - Arenas `solis-square` (the market square) and `solis-approach` (the road outside the main gate), each `{ center, retreatAxis }`. The combat rules only allow a retreat toward **+axis**; the enemy enters 12 to 19 m on the -axis side of the centre within 9 m across, the allies form up 10 to 14 m on the +axis side, the retreat line is 21 m out. So each arena needs a clear, collider-free, fairly level rectangle about 24 m across and 46 m along its axis, with the + end toward where the traveler comes from (the gate for the square, the city for the approach) and walkable ground just past the retreat line. Lay Solis out so this works: stalls, the fountain and carts go around the square's edges.
  - `tests/story-stands.test.js` checks every built site and arena for footing and a way out; it must pass with the Solis entries filled.
  - Envoy Telis Orren exists twice by design: your border chapter's envoy (before the fork) and `aftermath-envoy` (after the battle, Coalition side only). They must never be out at the same time, and after the fork the Empire's player must not find her in the hall.
- **Places change hands.** The user wants the map's politics to show on the ground: Solis becomes Ambroni if the Empire wins and clears it; the Legion's outpost becomes the Coalition's if the Republic wins and takes it. The lead provides `src/occupation.js` on `main` before you start: a person or prop with `holds: 'empire' | 'coalition'` and a `region` is out only while the campaign's control of that region says so. Give Solis both garrisons: the Coalition's (gate guards, captains, the camp outside the walls, contingent banners) with `holds: 'coalition'`, and a smaller Legion occupation (legionaries on the Gate of Sun Horses, a Tribune's clerk in the Court of Oaths, the Legate's standard where the Republic's flag flew, the Coalition's camp struck to bare tent rings) with `holds: 'empire'`. Townsfolk hold nothing and stay, with one line each that changes with the holder.
