# Introductory spell quests

Ben in Nothom teaches **Fireball** after the spider dies with Ben alive. Liz in Pueth teaches **Summon Bees** after Mop returns alive. Troy in Cobble teaches **Mind Read** after the player hears all three witnesses and identifies Torven Oss. Ben offers an explicit, mutually exclusive reward choice on one page: **35 copper** or **Fireball plus a spare wand**. The bounty does not subsequently grant a free lesson. Liz and Troy retain their existing coin-then-lesson options; payment and teaching each happen at most once.

Ben leads out through Nothom's north gate and into the woods. He walks around solid scenery, pauses when the player falls more than eleven metres behind, and resumes once they come within seven metres. Walking away does not make him follow the player back. Sela's unsolicited approach waits while Ben is leading an escort; manually speaking with her remains available. The den fight waits until both are nearby and Ben is conscious; Ben starts at his actual standing position. His guide waypoint and position persist in the existing optional `spider` checkpoint data. Retreating with him alive permits regrouping and another attempt; his death closes the quest in that timeline, including after retreat or reloading a save taken after his death. Restoring the pre-fight checkpoint restores him along with the rest of that earlier state. Older unfinished-fight saves resume as a walk. Loading an old walking save without guide data places Ben at a safe route stop near the saved player, or at his authored town position if no route stop is nearby. This migration only happens during loading.

Ben keeps his purple sorcerer's clothes and wand during the encounter. He holds his distance, telegraphs a cast, and launches a visible fireball that travels and damages the first body it hits; the player can be hit too. The giant spider has eight articulated legs, a visible rearing attack, and a matching corpse. Its solid footprint covers its shell rather than the full reach of its legs. Ordinary encounters do not show the old Greenway title or an enemy counter. Large skill introductions wait until combat ends, keeping their full reading time.

The traveler can run away from the den in any direction and return with Ben. Leaving and re-entering preserves player, Ben, and spider injuries. Death offers a full pre-fight checkpoint restoration or a return to safe ground that keeps current progress and losses. A separately saved adventure can also be loaded. None of these choices forces an immediate rematch. A temporary pre-fight checkpoint works during F8 testing without writing to the normal adventure save.

Ben puts his spare plain wand in the satchel without changing the equipped weapon. His lesson explains **I to open equipment, select Plain wand, then Equip**; a dialogue button opens the satchel directly. A repeated lesson cannot duplicate a wand or repair an existing broken one. Other teachers retain their existing first-focus assistance. Equip a wand or oak staff through the satchel. **Z casts**, **N changes the selected spell**, and the small right-hand spell panel provides buttons. Fireball travels along the player's facing, stops at scenery and damages the first person hit, including friends. Bees seek active enemy combatants, sting while the swarm lasts, then disperse. Mind Read needs a visible living person within four metres and displays their thought; Cobble witnesses retain their authored readings. Repeating a reading spends focus but does not farm experience.

Focus starts at 60 and returns outside combat. Fireball costs 20, bees 30, and Mind Read 25 at level one. Cast time, damage, cost and swarm duration scale with the appropriate school. Staff casts are slower and stronger. Interrupted casts still cost focus. The Skills browser groups **Fire Sorcery**, **Mind Sorcery**, and **Animal Sorcery** under **Sorcery**, with learned/not-learned status on each spell. Internal school IDs remain `fire`, `mind`, and `beast`, preserving existing XP and saves. School level alone does not unlock a spell; earned spells, selection, focus and previously read minds are saved separately in the optional `magic` checkpoint field. Older completed lesson saves migrate from the quest's `taught` stage.

`magic.js` owns spell timing and contacts. `combat.spellHit` owns encounter health, armour and defeat. Ordinary NPC spell contacts use the host's crime health pipeline exactly once. `magic-view.js` draws the projectiles and swarms; `magic-ui.js` shows focus and selection. Physical wand/staff strikes still use Staves; spell damage pays only its school.

Run `npm run test:magic` for model and real combat/escort tests. Run `npm run test:magic:desktop` for actual dialogue, frame triggers, Z input, rewards and checkpoint restoration in Electron. The desktop check uses spatial fixture warps, ordinary sword attacks and timed dodges. It checks Ben's leading, waiting, saved route, regrouping and permanent death, then forks the actual unrewarded victory snapshot to test both exclusive rewards. It verifies the explicit wand Equip action before Z can cast, all three Sorcery school labels, and coin-then-lesson saves for Liz and Troy. The pure integration tests additionally walk Ben through the solid town and woods and walk Mop all the way home. Spell targeting drops completed encounter rosters so a surviving NPC's current world position remains authoritative after a battle.

Run `npm run test:ben:desktop` for the additional live-renderer regression. It verifies Ben's actual sorcerer geometry, visible spider and moving fireball, projectile damage, ordinary movement out of the fight, regrouping, a real spider strike causing defeat, and the checkpoint button restoring safe play. It checks that testing recovery does not alter the saved adventure. Results and a representative rendered fight are written to `tests/artifacts/ben-fight-checks.json` and `tests/artifacts/ben-fight.png`. The test shortens the long walk with an approach fixture, but runs combat and guide movement on the normal renderer loop.

## Watching a complete Ben quest

Open **F8 → Quest playtests → Play Ben’s quest · Fireball**. The button starts a fresh Ben quest in the testing session, teleports to him in Nothom, and then the computer handles the conversation, follows him through the gate and woods, fights alongside him, and returns to Ben. **Autoplay stops with both rewards visible so the player chooses**; it cannot silently take either reward. Choosing the lesson then explains how to equip and cast. It supplies a repaired simple sword and five pawpaws for the test. The normal saved adventure is not replaced.

Any normal key or a click on the game takes control. **P** resumes the focused Ben quest, including an interrupted conversation. Use the F8 button again for a fresh run. A dead player, fallen Ben, unrelated encounter, or blocked progression stops the pilot and reports why; it does not grant victory or silently replay a failed fight.

`npm run test:ben:autoplay` runs the same button and full route in an isolated Electron profile. The check observes ordinary combat, handoff at the reward choice, an explicit player selection of the earned lesson, saved-adventure isolation, repeatability, and native WASD takeover. It does not teleport or advance quest state after the button starts. Results are in `tests/artifacts/ben-autoplay-checks.json`.

## Watching Liz and Troy's quests

Open **F8 > Quest playtests** and choose **Play Liz's quest · Summon Bees** or **Play Troy's quest · Mind Read**. Each button starts a fresh attempt beside that teacher. Liz's pilot approaches Mop quietly, waits for him to follow, and walks him home at his pace. Troy's pilot walks around Cobble, interviews all three witnesses, and returns with the evidence to name the murderer. Both use ordinary movement and dialogue; after the initial teleport, they do not move characters or award quest progress directly.

Both pilots stop with **money and the magic lesson visible**, leaving the reward to the player. Press a movement key or click the game to take control; **P** resumes the focused quest. Pause and menus stop their progress. An unavailable character or an unexpected fight stops autoplay with an explanation. Use the same F8 button again to restart that quest. Testing is visibly marked and leaves the normal saved adventure unchanged; close and reopen the game to return to normal play.

Mop now takes small frame-by-frame steps through the ordinary NPC movement system. His checkpoint records his actual position, following state, and any remaining hiding time. Older checkpoints remain compatible. If he is killed before returning, Liz's rescue ends rather than leaving an invisible cat to follow.

Run `npm run test:liz:autoplay` and `npm run test:troy:autoplay` separately for the full Electron routes. These checks exercise the F8 buttons, ordinary walking and conversations, pause, a checkpoint reload midway through the quest, the explicit lesson choice, restart, trusted keyboard takeover, and P resume. Test checkpoints use the temporary session store. Results are written to `tests/artifacts/liz-autoplay-checks.json` and `tests/artifacts/troy-autoplay-checks.json`.
