# Aevis and the open bronze harbor

Aevis follows the eastern shoreline of Southern Ascarth, centered near **(-921, 1890)** in world coordinates. The city has been moved southeast along the coastal bend and reshaped into a long, narrow strip. Its western wall leaves more than 100m of dry peninsula outside the city. The source atlas and coastline remain unchanged.

The **9,360 square metre** outline preserves essentially the original 9,400 square metre footprint and the dimensions of all ten buildings. It remains larger than Solis. The 18m landward curtains and 24m towers are lower than Nylon's intentionally enormous defenses. Five connected curtain edges protect every dry approach; both terminal towers stand offshore, with foundations reaching the seabed. A bronze gate faces inland and a second gate opens south onto the peninsula. The eastern waterfront faces open water throughout. Working quays, two piers, a merchant vessel and a war galley remain exposed to the sea.

The city's appearance follows the Ascarth lore: warm stone structure clad in golden-orange bronze, older verdigris panels, monumental bronze gates, and bull figures looking toward the water. The upper citadel is a broad megaron palace rather than Nylon's vertical civic towers. Its lower, wider neighboring building houses the *veth*, the keepers and public reciters of Avite heroic memory. A bronze guild, arsenal, barracks, shrine, lineage house, guardhouses and tin warehouse make the martial and metalworking character visible. Tin is imported; the exceptional Avite bronze is a developed craft, not a substitute for supposedly more advanced iron.

Eleven Avite soldiers demonstrate a distinct armor silhouette inspired by Mycenaean bronze cuirasses and greaves, broad plated shoulders, bronze neck protection and tall shields. Reference: [Mycenaean bronze weapons, Paul and Alexandra Canellopoulos Museum](https://camu.gr/en/item/mykinaika-chalkina-opla/). The reference informs the equipment, while local Avite culture and the game's existing character proportions determine the result. No civilian characters have been invented.

The terrain grades continuously from the inland citadel down to the quay. Foundations accommodate its modest slope. Streets, approaches, monuments and soldier stands stay clear of building collisions. Timber decks provide a separate 3.2 m walking surface over existing water; they do not turn the sea into land. A paved ramp connects the quay to the streets. City and road reservations keep later streamed vegetation and terrestrial wildlife out of the built area.
