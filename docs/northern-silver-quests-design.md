# Northern silver quests and the undead advance

Updated 5 October 2026. Northern Azhora gains a gradually available series of silver-grade political quests alongside the southern rebel-versus-monarchist quests. The first major human targets of the undead lord on **Eshtor Plateau** are **Witherst and Riesov**. Their immediate struggle is to protect people and settlements from the duke's expanding power. Thalmagar's role emerges through investigation rather than being announced by the first quest.

This is planned quest design, not an implemented registry or a set of playable quests. The overarching enemy, secret vassals and territorial changes follow [Thalmagar crisis and the Forsaken Citadel](thalmagar-crisis-design.md). The [existing civil-war quest series](ambroni-civil-war.md) supplies a useful precedent for local political consequences without automatic province conquest.

## The northern situation

The Forsaken Citadel's undead faction presses outward early in the game, spreading the fictional plague and undead influence. Witherst and Riesov are the first major human kingdoms affected because of their proximity to Eshtor. The advance is slow and persistent, consuming vulnerable communities over a long part of the campaign if no effective resistance intervenes. This regional offensive can unfold while Thalmagar himself is still concentrating on fortification, mustering and limited expansion elsewhere.

Use exact atlas regions **West Witherst, East Witherst, North Riesov and South Riesov** when placing quests. Which particular communities and routes suffer first remains to be authored. Cold Stones remains part of Witherst's earlier territorial claim, but it is not automatically a new plague outbreak or the first battlefield. The Citadel's exact location within Eshtor is also still to be chosen.

The duke, all four independent Goblinland factions and the West and East Baldro dwarf states are mutually at peace, sharing opposition to human expansion. Their agreements keep the duke from expanding by conquest into his goblin and dwarf neighbors. He favors the human lands to the east: Witherst and Riesov, then East Ganun and the rest of Ganun. Thoth is another opening, reached on a separate branch through West Witherst rather than directly from Ganun. These are strategic preferences, not guaranteed conquests or fixed marching orders. Peace does not by itself grant military transit or prove that every partner secretly serves Thalmagar. North Gorgi's secret vassalage is established separately; its three goblin allies are not thereby his vassals.

The latest dictated "Ysabeau" is provisionally read as Riesov from the preceding northern-kingdom context; there is no matching atlas region. "Withhurst" and "Withurst" follow the established Witherst name. No new kingdom is created by these transcription variants.

Local action can save people, preserve supplies and hold important ground. Sustained resistance requires intervention by more distant human kingdoms. Winning that help is a political task: less exposed rulers may doubt reports, protect their own interests or disagree about their obligations. The High King can convene or recognize a response without having direct command over every nominal vassal's army.

This regional struggle also affects the later authored invasion. Thalmagar wants the undead to secure much of the north before North Gorgi's powerful army completes its long muster. Saving kingdoms now can preserve resistance and deny that army an uncontested approach later. The confirmed Lond–Endevor–Acor corridor requires its own control and access; losing Riesov and Ganun alone does not automatically open it. Quest briefings reveal this larger connection only as the hero acquires evidence.

## Gradual availability and discovery

**Ithzel adds a second early intervention front.** The returned human Lower King holds East Ithzel against the Blood Prince's remaining western undead faction after Wilhelm leaves with his main army. The starting balance is roughly even, and hero action can tip it. An undead victory threatens Ganun sooner from this island front; a human victory can curtail that foothold. Eshtor remains the stronger undead faction. See the [island conflict](thalmagar-crisis-design.md#the-blood-princes-island-faction) for the link to Wilhelm's later southern commitment. The intended player opening is now Minora.

Quests become available as events create needs and evidence, rather than all appearing at game start or depending only on character level. Rumors, a missing convoy, a returning patrol, an approaching force, a displaced community or corroborated testimony can open the next opportunity.

The proposed discovery sequence is:

1. **Local trouble:** people are frightened, movement is unsafe and supplies or neighbors go missing. Explanations can be uncertain.
2. **Organized undead activity:** credible encounters or witnesses establish that disguised undead are involved. Knowing that does not identify every hidden agent.
3. **The Citadel's role:** evidence connects events to the duke on Eshtor and challenges his public claim to act for Ganun.
4. **Hidden allegiance:** sufficiently strong evidence can expose his service to Thalmagar. This is distinct from discovering that the duke is undead.
5. **Collective response:** the hero turns evidence, relationships and practical requests into distant assistance.

This sequence describes increasing understanding, not a rigid requirement that all six proposed quests below must be completed in order. A force can attack before the hero understands its superior. Aid can be requested against a demonstrated local danger before anyone proves Thalmagar's involvement. The world continues if these quests are ignored.

## Quests can be lost to the advance

These opportunities depend on living communities and people. Before a threatened place falls, the hero can help its inhabitants resist, obtain relief or escape. If those people are conquered and transformed into hostile undead, their original quests become unavailable. A former quest giver can later be encountered as an enemy, retaining the same identity and the history of what happened. The campaign does not keep their old errands available indefinitely while the hero does other things.

Territorial conquest, infection, death, raising and hostile allegiance remain separate world events. A border changing color does not instantly convert every inhabitant. Some people may still be living under occupation; others may have escaped, died or been transformed. Quest availability follows the actual people, objectives and access conditions, including loss before the hero ever accepts the quest.

Record an accepted quest's lost or unavailable outcome without awarding completion rewards or respawning its giver. The journal describes only what the hero has learned: a missing contact need not immediately reveal that person's hidden undead identity. Later evidence can establish the final fate. Do not advertise undiscovered secret allegiances through a quest status change.

Early defense, relief and evacuation can preserve people and future relationships. Taking a settlement back does not automatically cure its transformed population or reopen their old quests. A later rescue, reclamation or confrontation can be separately authored, but a lost quest is not guaranteed a replacement. Exact plague rates, warning periods and particular quest deadlines remain to be designed.

## Proposed quest hooks

The following titles and specific premises are working proposals. Settlement names, people, route sites and numerical rewards are not yet assigned.

| Working title | Initial area and trigger | Hero work | Durable local or campaign consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Missing Winter Grain** | Witherst; an expected shipment fails to arrive | Find the actual convoy, rescue its crew, recover grain or negotiate a replacement | Delivered cargo replenishes a real settlement or militia store. An investigation may uncover suspicious Eshtor purchases without revealing Thalmagar |
| **The Road Out** | Threatened Witherst or Riesov communities; traffic or escape is being cut off | Escort travelers, find the force blocking passage, negotiate access or clear a route | Surviving people arrive at a named destination; route access and throughput change. Lost cargo and casualties remain lost |
| **Those Who Returned** | Witherst or Riesov; witnesses recognize someone believed dead among a patrol or attackers | Investigate identity, protect witnesses and obtain corroboration | Evidence establishes organized undead activity and changes what specific authorities know. It does not automatically expose the duke's overlord |
| **Hold the Northern Road** | Witherst or Riesov; a known force approaches an authored crossing or defensive site | Help provision and defend it, repair a real crossing, or organize evacuation if it cannot be held | The holding, route and civilian outcome change separately. Losing the position need not erase a successful evacuation |
| **Whose Orders** | After credible links to Eshtor emerge; investigation can extend to Ganun's representatives | Trace a courier, orders or supply arrangements and test the duke's purported authority | Authorities may permit inspection, restrict his support or commit defensive forces. Proof of a Ganun claim being abused is separate from proof of Thalmagar's vassalage |
| **A Hearing for the North** | A more distant human court or the Stone Fist's court; sufficient reports and a concrete request exist | Bring testimony, reconcile political objections and negotiate help | A stated relief force, grain shipment, access agreement or other commitment is created with conditions and travel time. Distant assistance must actually reach the threatened kingdoms |
| **The Divided Island** | Ithzel; the returned king's eastern restoration contests Wilhelm's western holdings | Support local defense, expose undead influence or secure relief for the restoration | Actual aid and local victories can tip the balanced conflict; neither whole-island liberation nor the undead outcome is guaranteed by accepting a quest |

These quests allow combat, investigation, escort, relief and political negotiation to contribute to the same strategic struggle. The player can work for different aligned factions. Evidence and promises attach to particular people and governments rather than producing automatic universal agreement.

## Outcomes and political consequences

A quest should explain what it accomplished: how many actual survivors arrived, which route reopened, whose testimony is trusted, or what a ruler committed. Exact quantities follow the authored encounter and logistics; this design assigns none in advance.

A local success does not instantly pacify a region, cure the plague everywhere or make an entire kingdom join the player's coalition. It can build a relationship that supports a later negotiation. Conversely, refusing to help or losing a defense can change local conditions without arbitrarily failing the whole campaign.

If an event overtakes a quest, its state follows the world, including permanent closure when its people become hostile undead. A conquered town may support a separately authored relief or evacuation opportunity if living people remain. A dead witness remains dead unless explicitly raised; another source of evidence is possible only if it exists in the world. Returning to campaign mode or reloading must not restore a lost convoy, respawn a witness, reopen a closed quest or award the same supplies twice.

Campaign-only players can respond strategically to reported invasions, allocate aid and negotiate against known threats. Hero investigation remains the principal means of exposing concealed factions and vassal ties; the exact alternative intelligence routes remain open. Merely viewing fully revealed geography does not provide the evidence.

## Limits still to resolve

- The duke's initial forces, slow expansion rates, exact first targets and usable routes toward Ganun and Thoth.
- Quest sites, speakers, prerequisites, political choices and rewards.
- Disguise tells and what evidence establishes identity, faction membership or vassalage.
- Plague transmission, treatment and prevention. None of these hooks assumes an established cure, immunity rule or automatic spread mechanism.
- Which distant kings offer help, what they demand and how their forces are commanded.
- How early intervention changes the northern offensive and Thalmagar's later crisis.

The existing main map should eventually display those actual developments, while the ordinary diplomatic view preserves undiscovered secret ties. The series provides the northern route from helping threatened people to building the alliance required by the overarching campaign.
