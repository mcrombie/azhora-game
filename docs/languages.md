# The tongues of Azhora, and the linguist skill

Nobody in this world speaks the traveler's language.

That is the whole feature. What a person says to you arrives in their own tongue
and stays there until you have heard enough of it. As you learn, more of every
line arrives in English, until at the end there is nothing on the continent you
cannot follow. It is texture, never a puzzle: your own lines are always English,
and so are the quest panel, the journal and every toast, because those are your
own notes. Nothing you have to do is ever gated on reading a language.

This document is the record of every default chosen. Where the lore settled
something it says so; where nothing settled it, it says that too, and says what
should be rounded out next.

**Code.** `src/languages.js` (the tongues, their sound, who speaks them, the word
forge), `src/linguist.js` (proficiency, exposure, rendering, the save),
`src/word-frequency.js` (generated by `scripts/build-word-frequency.mjs`).
Tests: `tests/languages.test.js`, `tests/linguist.test.js`.

---

## 1. What the traveler knows on the first morning

**Nothing.** Not one word of Ambroni, which is the language of the contract he
came on, and nothing of Drentish, which is what Tidehaven speaks.
`STARTING_PROFICIENCY` in `src/languages.js` is a table of fourteen zeros; it is
a table so it can be tuned in one place without touching any rule.

What makes the opening followable is **Chris Scotwood**. He came off the same
boat, he carries the Empire's letter, and he has enough of the local speech to
get two men up a road. While he is beside you:

- the line is shown twice — the speaker's words as you hear them, and under
  them, in an aside in his name, what it meant;
- every exposure counts **double**, so sticking with him early is the fast road.

He knows **Ambroni, Drentish and Feradom** and not the world. Once the company
musters on the Moros he stops walking with anybody, the aside stops, and
everything after that is yours to learn. That is the shape of the skill: every
new country is a new climb from wherever that tongue stands, and the far ones —
the islands, the fens, the highland passes — climb no faster than the first did.

**Chris himself always speaks plainly.** He came off the same boat on the same
contract; whatever the traveler's own head speaks, Chris speaks it. He is the
one person in Azhora who does, and it is the reason the first hour of the road
works at all. `speechFor` gives him no tongue, so his lines arrive whole and
teach nothing — there is nothing in them to learn. (His origin is Feradom and
that is still what `ORIGIN_LANGUAGE` says; it is simply not what he speaks
to you.)

`INTERPRETER` holds the rest: the npc id, the three tongues, twelve metres, the
`mustered` phase and the doubling. `linguist.interpreterNearby(npc, …)` is the
one predicate — measured from Chris to the traveler, who is the one being spoken
to — and a companion system can replace its body with "is recruited" without
touching anything else.

## 2. The fourteen tongues

Fourteen, which is four more than the ten the brief guessed at, for a reason:
the fourteen playable regions need **seven** between them, and the hired company
brings **seven** more ashore, because it was called from abroad and every one of
them came from somewhere.

`from` says where each sound comes from: the world-builder's own naming profiles
in `../world-builder/azhoran_language_profiles.py` where one exists (nine of the
sixteen are used), and derivation from the lore plus the region's own authored
place names where one does not.

### The seven of the atlas

| Tongue | Who and where | Sound from | Lore |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ambroni** (endonym *Elagosi*) | Elagos and the Lake Lands, and every clerk, prefect and soldier the Empire posts anywhere | profile `elagosi` | Its own family, related to nothing else anyone has proved; the prestige administrative tongue of eastern Azhora in strong imperial periods |
| **Drentish** | Drent, Pueth, the Pebbles | derived: Drent's authored names (Drent, Rena, Avrel, Caloss, the Torn) over Elagosi, which it has stood beside for centuries | "Its own thing… related to Elagosi in the way neighbouring peoples in contact for centuries develop similarities without becoming the same" |
| **Mittoli** | Luscia, the Moros Plain, Amod, Vastos, Meneth, Caricas, Nesdor, Eer, Isareos | profile `mittoli` | The great western family; commerce, governance and scholarship |
| **Koleth** | East Suval: Elod, its harbour quarter, its country | profile `elodi` | Not an Azhoran language at all — it crossed the Iberos Sea with the Elodi migration. *Koleth* means the common tongue |
| **Suvalen** | West Suval: Solis, the downs, the fishing coast | derived: coastal Mittoli, with the glosses the game already uses at Vaervelm Caelazh | "A variety of the Iberos coastal contact language, related to mainland trade Mittoli" |
| **Izoli** | the island of Izol | derived: the island's own names (Izolveth, Ardveth, Kelvath, Anvath, Doreth) and the goddess's domain | The coastal dialect trades; the highland variety is conservative and does not translate its shrine vocabulary |

That is six rows for seven tongues because Mittoli covers seven regions through
its dialects.

### The seven the company brought

| Tongue | Who | Sound from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Feradom speech** | Chris Scotwood, Ciarán | derived: Drentish diverged, with the cold clusters of profile `crefs` |
| **Maroshi** | Lakota, of the Marosh fens | profile `moreshi` |
| **Pyrosi** | Eliana, of the Pyrosi hills | profile `pyrosi` |
| **Selemi** | Kristen, of the Selemi coast | derived: profile `tennoca`, whose coastal-trading sound fits the Iberos Sea's own maritime power |
| **Kellith** | Matt, Prince of Zorkys | profile `kellith` — the highland-passes tongue, for a man with a hall, a valley and four hundred people |
| **Bouéni** | Al the Tun, of the southern islands | profile `boueni` |
| **Harbour Cant** | Ed the Word, of no port he will name | a pidgin: every word is forged in whichever donor tongue the word itself picks, over a dozen of its own |
| **Ibnael** | Mus, of nowhere he has said | profile `ibnael` — Forest Mittoli, and under it the substrate no Azhoran linguist has placed |

Eight rows, because Harbour Cant and Ibnael are the two that needed a decision:

- **Ed the Word** has no country, so he gets the thing that has no country. Harbour
  Cant is what four centuries of Iberos coastal trade left in the ports, and the
  lore supports it directly: East Pyrosi as "more vocabulary, less grammar,
  maximum utility", Iberos terms in three separate Azhoran families. It has no
  phonotactics of its own — each word comes out of a donor tongue chosen by the
  word — and it is the only tongue that takes a floor from *everything* you know
  (a quarter of your best), because a traveler who has been anywhere
  half-understands a quay. That is what a pidgin is for.
- **Mus** says "I do not use the road. It goes where everybody knows it goes",
  and walks to the muster through the woods. He speaks **Ibnael** — Forest
  Mittoli, whose substrate has words for particular qualities of forest light,
  categories of forest silence, the behaviour of old trees and the felt presence
  of unseen things, with no cognates anywhere. Nobody can place his accent
  because Azhoran linguists cannot place it either.

### Dialects

A dialect shares its parent's proficiency — a Pebble pilot and a Drent carter
understand each other — and changes only the sound. Word order never moves.

| Dialect | Of | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the Pueth accent | Drentish | `-en` → `-in`, `-oss` → `-os`; fewer Elagosi loanwords, the occupation there being shorter |
| Pebble Drentish | Drentish | Drops an internal vowel in anything over five letters: the redundancy squeezed out by people who talk in known contexts |
| Luscian Mittoli | Mittoli | Elagosi administrative loanwords, Moros commercial vocabulary, steppe land-use terms |
| the Plain's Mittoli | Mittoli | Doubles a final vowel; an older layer under its place names that derives from no identified neighbour |
| Amodian | Mittoli | An apostrophe where two vowels meet: the terrace country's clipped water words |
| Vastos Mittoli | Mittoli | Broadens `o` to `ou`; Mittoli-contact speech over a pastoral core (the *vel-vastos*, the year's movement of herds) |
| Meneth Mittoli | Mittoli | Final `-th` → `-nth`, the pre-Mittoli Lotharn cluster the name *Meneth* itself comes out of |
| Carican Mittoli | Mittoli | Final `-as`/`-os` → `-ac`/`-oc`; inner-branch speech with its own vocabulary for fox-corridor practice |
| Nesdor Mittoli | Mittoli | Rolls an `r`; Plains Mittoli facing the Moros, named from *nessar* (counted) + *dorath* (water-place) |
| Eer Mittoli | Mittoli | Shortens a doubled `e`; coastal-transitional, a stratigraphic record of every power that has administered the place, and place names that describe the ground and commemorate nobody |
| Isareos Mittoli | Mittoli | Final `-os` → `-eos`; the western-interior speech of the valley heads, carrying **the ford vocabulary** — single terms for water heights and crossing conditions that Standard Mittoli needs a compound for |
| the highland Izoli | Izoli | Doubles a final consonant; conservative, and keeps shrine vocabulary the towns do not have |

**The four western regions** — Vastos, Meneth, Caricas, Nesdor — are all Mittoli
dialects rather than Ambroni, because the lore says so in each case ("Mittoli-derived
in language", "Carican inner-branch Mittoli", "the base dialect is Plains
Mittoli", and Vastos's *vel-vastos* is called "the Mittoli-contact word"). They
sit inside the Empire's reach, so the people speak Mittoli and the prefect, the
clerk and the soldier answer in Ambroni — which is exactly what `IMPERIAL_ROLES`
does.

**Eer and Isareos** are the same answer again, from their own lore files: Eer speaks
"a Mittoli dialect that linguists categorize as coastal-transitional" and Isareos
"the western-interior dialect of Standard Mittoli, with the ford vocabulary". Neither
needed a new tongue. **A region added to the atlas needs a line in `REGION_LANGUAGE`**
or `tests/languages.test.js` goes red, which is the point: it is a question for the
lore and not something to default. Nethereum, Ovesos, the Oves Desert and Gala are next.

## 3. Who speaks what, in order

`speechFor(npc, region)`:

1. an explicit `npc.language` (with optional `npc.dialect`) wins;
2. then `npc.origin`, through `ORIGIN_LANGUAGE` — a mercenary carries his own
   tongue up the road whatever country he is standing in;
3. then `IMPERIAL_ROLES` — the Empire's own people answer in Ambroni anywhere;
4. then the ground, through `REGION_LANGUAGE`.

Every one of the fourteen regions and every one of the nine mercenary origins
has an entry, and tests fail if either gains a member without one.

## 4. The seed lexicons

Where the lore glosses a real root, that root makes the tongue's real word and
the forge never sees that English word again. The seeds:

- ***cael*** water, to flow, and ***azh*** to endure — from `src/winery.js`,
  which already explains *Vaervelm Caelazh* as *vaer* (good) + *velm* (green,
  fertile place) + *cael* + *-azh*. Suvalen and Mittoli both take them.
- ***ael*** speech — from *Talaelos*, "fiery speech", in `src/troupe.js`.
  Mittoli and Ibnael.
- ***veth*** the bond that holds — every Izoli town is *-veth* or *-vath*, and
  Izol's own domain is "what keeps the rock from becoming sand and a promise
  from becoming words". Izoli's word for hold, keep, bond, oath and promise.
- ***nessar*** counted, measured — the Academy's reconstruction of *Nesdor* as
  *nessar* + *dorath*. Mittoli.
- ***vorn*** sky and ***ond*** broad water — Ibnael onsets in the profile.
- ***kol*** word, tongue — *Koleth*, "the common tongue".
- ***gal*** crossing — the lore's one attested Mittoli–Iberos cognate.
- ***hom*** house — *Tharganhom*, the Wine Attic of Solis.

Each tongue then takes its fifteen `lexical_roots` concepts from its profile —
settlement, river, forest, hill, plain, marsh, sea, border, market, fort, sacred,
sun, ancestor, ruler, dynasty — built into words with its own suffixes, plus
whatever else the country needs (Izoli has *assembly* and *speaker*, Feradom has
*pass* and *ridge*, Maroshi has *well* and *wind*, Kellith has *hall* and *valley*).

## 5. How a line is rendered

1. **Tokenise.** Everything that is not a word — punctuation, numbers, spacing,
   ampersands, dashes — comes back exactly as it went in.
2. **Names pass.** A name is a name in every language. A token is a name if it
   is in `PLACE_NAMES` (every region and charted subregion on the atlas, the
   tongues themselves, and `EXTRA_NAMES` for the people and grapes the chart
   does not record), or if the NPC roster knows it, or if it is capitalised away
   from the start of a sentence and is not one of the forty-five commonest words
   in the game. A whole line that is a Roman numeral is a number: the milestones
   on the Moros count the same miles for everybody. A possessive is tested on its
   stem, so *Sava's Shrine* keeps Sava.
3. **Known or not, by rank.** Every other word is English if it is one you have
   learned. Which words those are is **principled, not random**: `costOf(word)`
   is the share of running speech the words *commoner than it* account for, and a
   word is known when that share is below your comprehension. So you learn the
   commonest words first and a word once known stays known — a test walks every
   level from 0 to 99 and fails if anything is ever forgotten.
4. **Otherwise, the tongue's word.** A stable hash of tongue plus lowercase word
   seeds a generator over that tongue's onsets, middles and suffixes, kept near
   the length of the English it stands for so a rendered line still has the shape
   of a sentence somebody said. Capitalisation and the possessive `'s` are put
   back. The dialect's twist runs last.
5. **Never reorder.**

### The curve

`comprehension(level)` is **smoothstep on level/99**: `t² (3 − 2t)`.

| level | of a line in English |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 0% |
| 10 | 3% |
| 20 | 11% |
| 30 | 22% |
| 40 | 36% |
| 50 | 51% |
| 60 | 66% |
| 70 | 79% |
| 80 | 90% |
| 90 | 98% |
| 99 | 100% |

Slow at first, most of the ground in the middle, and the whole of it only at 99,
because the last words anybody learns are the ones nobody says twice.

### The frequency list

`src/word-frequency.js` is generated by `node scripts/build-word-frequency.mjs`,
which walks every string literal in `src/` that looks like prose and counts the
words: **94,220 spoken words, 6,043 distinct**, of which the top 5,000 are kept
and cover 98.9% of everything anybody says. "Commonest" has to mean commonest
*here* — this game says *goblin*, *satchel*, *quay* and *quartermaster* rather
more than English at large does.

The shape is what matters: **the ten commonest words are a quarter of all speech,
the first hundred are half, the first thousand are 85%.** That is why twenty per
cent of a line reads as scaffolding rather than as a fifth of a random sentence.

Regenerate it when a lot of new dialogue lands. Nothing breaks if it goes stale:
a word the count has not heard of is simply one of the last you learn.

## 6. How you learn

- Every line heard in a tongue is **exposure** in it.
- The k-th line from the same mouth is worth `1/√k`, with a floor of 0.12. One
  person cannot teach you their language — forty lines from one villager is a
  beginning and not a language — which forces breadth. You learn Elagosi by
  talking to Elagosi, plural.
- Chris Scotwood's presence doubles it.
- **900 exposure is fluency**, on `99·ln(1+e/60)/ln(16)`: 30 exposure is level
  14, 100 is 35, 300 is 63, 600 is 86, 900 is exactly 99. Every level round-trips
  through its inverse, which is how the starting table would seed a non-zero
  tongue if you ever want one.

### Kinship

A relative gives you a **floor and never a ceiling**, one hop and never through
a third. Knowing Drentish at 60 puts 27 under Feradom: you catch the shape of it
without ever having heard a word. The numbers come from the lore's own account:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Drentish ↔ Feradom | 0.45 — "long-separated cousins, recognisably the same source" |
| Mittoli ↔ Suvalen | 0.40 — a coastal contact variety of it |
| Mittoli ↔ Ibnael | 0.35 — Ibnael *is* Forest Mittoli, under its substrate |
| Mittoli ↔ Pyrosi | 0.30 — Proto-Western, and in contact ever since |
| Pyrosi ↔ Kellith | 0.30 — Telemon sits on the East Pyros edge |
| Suvalen ↔ Selemi | 0.25 — Selemi commercial vocabulary in Sorveth |
| Drentish ↔ Ambroni | 0.20 — centuries of administration |
| Selemi ↔ Izoli | 0.20 — the outpost in Izolveth |
| Mittoli ↔ Bouéni | 0.15 — the most distant living relative |
| Ambroni ↔ Mittoli, Ambroni ↔ Feradom | 0.12 — administrative loanwords |
| Suvalen ↔ Koleth | 0.10 — the Elodi translate for commerce and offer no more |
| Maroshi | nothing. Its own lineage, older than the western families |
| Harbour Cant | a quarter of your **best** tongue, from anywhere |

### The phrasebook

Wendel sells one for **14 copper** (the starting purse is 24). Buying it is
reading it: the tongue of the country you bought it in, worth 60 exposure scaled
by how much of that tongue you *lack*, so it is worth less the more you know and
nothing at all to a fluent speaker. It stays in the satchel as a keepsake.

### The drills

The hook above is taken: Chris Scotwood teaches **Ambroni** on the long road through
Drent, in five sittings of **35 exposure** each, one to close each leg
(`docs/drent-long-road.md` §6, `DRILLS` in `src/long-road.js`). A drill is six
lines of the army's own speech with what each one means underneath, and there is
no quiz at the end of it: the player is never asked to produce a word.

The six lines are written in English and *rendered* through `renderLine` in full,
so what the traveler is shown is the same Ambroni everybody else in the game
speaks rather than anything invented for the lesson. What a drill adds is the
gloss, and the gloss is what makes it teaching instead of overhearing.

175 taught, with the seventeen or so heard on the road, is **Ambroni 51** by the
time the traveler crosses the Caloss — which is to say the army's signs turn
readable about as he leaves Drent. Walk the short road instead and it is about 7.
When the traveler *is* Chris the same five run the other way round: he gives them
to whoever came off his boat, and they pay the same, because giving a lesson in a
tongue is how anybody keeps one.

## 7. The traveler's own skill

`linguist`, on the RuneScape table to 99, registered in `src/skills.js` by
whoever owns that file. The traveler picks it up the first time a word he cannot
follow is said to him; until the entry exists this is a no-op and the tongues go
on being learned regardless.

- **120 experience per exposure** — a little for every line;
- **200 × level per level any tongue climbs** — a great deal for real progress.

Set so that **every tongue in Azhora carried to fluency is a 99 linguist**
(14 × 4,950 × 200 ≈ 13.9M against the table's 13,034,431). That is what the skill
means, and `tests/linguist.test.js` says so.

## 8. In the game

**The dialogue panel.** `openDialogue` records which tongue is being spoken;
`updateSpeech` renders the current line through the linguist at display time and
counts the exposure once per line shown, however often the panel is redrawn. The
review log keeps the English. Under the line, when Chris is beside you, his
interpretation in an italic aside (`#speech-aside`).

The speaker's **name** and **role** above the line stay English, as do the
choices below it. Those are the traveler's own labelling and the traveler's own
mouth; only the words other people say to him move.

**The toggle.** **T**, in dialogue only, flips between the line as you hear it
and the line as it was actually said. The caption under the panel says
`· T in Drentish` or `· T as you hear it`. It lasts the session and is not
saved, because it is a way of looking rather than a thing you have.

**The signs.** A sign stands in a country and is lettered in that country's
tongue until you can read it — proficiency **50**, `SIGN_READING_LEVEL` — and
then in yours. A toast says when a tongue crosses.

That is **all or nothing**, not the spectrum a spoken line gets, for two reasons.
The lettering atlas in `src/signs.js` is built once from a fixed list and every
board is cut to the width of what is written on it, so a sign with a different
word on it every few levels would need an unbounded atlas. And it is simply
better: a half-translated sentence is scaffolding, a half-translated three-word
sign is noise. Reading otherwise uses the same proficiency as hearing; there is
no second curve to keep in step.

The Empire's own furniture — the camp, the outpost, the stockade, the orders
nailed to it, the miles counted along its road — letters in **Ambroni** wherever
it stands, because the Empire laid it. `SIGN_LANGUAGE` holds the table. **A label
with no entry letters in English**, deliberately: a sign put up by somebody
working elsewhere in the game reads plainly until whoever knows that country says
what it should say, rather than breaking the road.

**Cost.** The atlas grows from 94 labels to 153 and its texture from 1024×4096
to 1536×4096 — 16 MB to 24 MB. That is the one place this feature costs memory.
`SIGN_TRANSLATION = false` in `src/languages.js` turns the whole of it off and
puts the atlas back.

**And normal mode pays none of it.** Since the mode gate (`docs/hard-mode.md`), the atlas is cut
for the labels that will actually be shown. `setForeignLettering(false)` — what `src/main.js`
says in normal mode, before the world is built — cuts it for the **95** English labels alone:
1536×2048, **12 MB**. With the foreign lettering in, **154** labels, 1536×4096, **24 MB**. So
normal mode gives twelve megabytes back, and the same switch settles `signText`, so a word the
atlas was not cut for can never be asked of it. `src/signs.js` is told yes or no and never learns
what a mode is.

**The save.** One line in `checkpoint.save`, one in restore, and
`validateLinguistSnapshot` in `src/road-checkpoint.js` beside the others. What is
saved is the exposure per tongue and how many lines each speaker has said; the
toggle is not, because it is a way of looking rather than a thing you have.

The speaker tally is bounded twice, because the checkpoint has 64 KB and the
world has a great many people in it. A count stops at **80**, past which the
weight is at its floor and the exact number means nothing; and the save keeps
the **900** speakers heard most. Somebody you met twice and lost costs you
nothing — their next line is worth 1 instead of 0.71 — where somebody you have
listened to all afternoon would otherwise be worth all of it again, which is the
one thing this must not give away. A save of 1,300 speakers is about 14 KB.

**Testing sessions.** Under `?test=1` `linguist.fluent()` sets every tongue at
once and pays no experience for it, so that the Electron smoke tests can go on
reading what people say to check the *content* of it — a dozen of them assert on
`$('speech').textContent` — without a boot full of level-up cards.
`window.__AZHORA__.linguist` offers `forget()`, `study(id, exposure)`,
`toggle(force)` and `view()`, so a review can put the traveler back to nothing
and photograph the panel as a new player sees it.

## 10. What still needs rounding out

1. **Ibnael has one speaker in the built world.** Mus, and nobody else. Per-speaker
   diminishing returns mean one man can never teach you a language, so as things
   stand his tongue is the one you will never have. That is good texture and bad
   arithmetic: it makes the linguist 99 unreachable until the western deep forest
   has people in it, or until somebody teaches Ibnael. A phrasebook for it would
   be wrong — Wendel does not stock a language nobody has placed. A teacher, or
   the forest, is the answer.
2. **The Selemi, Kellith and Bouéni tongues are one mercenary each**, for the same
   reason. Kristen, Matt and Al talk plenty, but a single mouth caps out. These
   want either countrymen in a later region or a teacher.
3. **No teachers exist.** `linguist.study(id, n)` is the hook; nothing is authored.
   The obvious first one is the Elodi, who "translate for commercial purposes"
   and do not offer their language — so a teacher in Koleth should cost something
   other than money.
4. **Sign lettering is a snapshot.** Signs take their language when the world is
   built, so crossing the reading threshold mid-session changes the road at the
   next load rather than immediately. A toast says so. Rebuilding sign geometry
   live is possible and was not worth it.
5. **`SIGN_LANGUAGE` needs a line per new sign label.** An unmapped label letters
   in English, which is safe, but a new region's signs will read plainly until
   somebody says what country they are in.
6. **Dialects are sound only.** The lore has more to say — West Pyrosi encodes
   social relationship in the verb, Amodian has water-measure words that describe
   behaviour and not quantity, the highland Izoli keeps shrine vocabulary
   untranslated. None of that is modelled; word order never moves and no register
   is distinguished. A per-tongue *register* (a formal and a plain form of the
   same line) is the next real thing to build here.
7. **The traveler's own speech.** His choices are always English, deliberately.
   Whether a person he is fluent in *should* hear him differently — whether the
   choices should ever say "(in Drentish)" — is a decision nobody has made.
8. **Koleth's scriptural form** exists in the lore (the texts in the innermost
   room of the Threshold, which the priesthood spends years on) and not in the
   game. If the Threshold is ever entered, that is a second Koleth with its own
   proficiency.
9. **Nothing here touches combat, the map, the journal or the quest panel.** By
   design. If the user ever wants the *journal* to record a tongue's progress,
   `linguist.view()` and `linguist.task()` are already shaped for a panel.
