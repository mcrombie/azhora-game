# Thalmagar crisis and the Forsaken Citadel

Updated 6 October 2026. Thalmagar is the overarching enemy of the campaign and the source of its eventual large-scale crisis. The hero's main strategic task is to assemble enough collective strength to oppose him while participating in the wars and politics already unfolding. The [campaign and hero modes](campaign-opening-design.md) share this world and its consequences.

The Forsaken Citadel is on **Eshtor Plateau**. Its former proposed placement in North Ganun is superseded. Eshtor belongs to the separate undead faction, replacing its earlier assignment to Goblinland. The location change moves the duke's seat and the Citadel concept to Eshtor; it does not place a second Citadel in Ganun.

## The overarching campaign

Thalmagar is the overarching strategic enemy in the resistance campaign being developed. He is a necromancer as well as a military and political threat. His own species, origins and possible undeath remain undecided. The player potentially becoming his servant and winning on his side is retained as a future design option; it does not yet supply an alternate quest line, victory condition or diplomacy system.

| Campaign stage | Thalmagar's direction | The player's strategic problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early | Fortify, muster forces and consolidate his base; make limited territorial gains | Understand the surrounding conflicts, choose allegiances and recognize the developing threat |
| Middle | Continue preparation, seek partners or subordinate rulers among the older peoples, and expand cautiously | Build a viable coalition or realm, contest his influence and decide which neighboring disputes to settle through alliance or conquest |
| Middle to late | Launch a major offensive intended to smash through opposing realms | Bring the accumulated alliances, armies, supply and territorial preparations to bear against a crisis facing the human kingdoms |

These stages form an authored scenario progression within a dynamic world. Its intended baseline is slow northern expansion and a long North Gorgi muster, followed by a major southern invasion. The player and other factions can change the strength, territory and circumstances of each stage. Exact dates and escalation conditions remain to be designed; the later sections establish the intended route and strategic objectives. The world does not wait for the hero to finish a checklist: neighboring rulers fight, borders change, and regions can be conquered during ordinary play. See the [scenario structure](campaign-opening-design.md#an-authored-scenario-in-a-dynamic-world).

Thalmagar's Empire directly controls **Cape Thalmagar, Urubond, Narcosh, Cudon and Lesser Oremindi Mountains** at the campaign opening. Cape Thalmagar remains his public seat and Urubond his secret true base. The Forsaken Citadel is the seat of a distinct subordinate undead ruler, not a replacement for Urubond or a second name for the Cape fortress.

## The hero builds the opposition

The hero cannot defeat the Dark Lord's armies alone. The main quest requires bringing sufficient factions and resources into effective cooperation. The player can pursue this through different political allegiances and combinations of diplomacy, local service, negotiation and conquest; a single predetermined alliance roster is not established.

Helping ordinary people and resolving local conflicts creates relationships that matter to this larger struggle. Some quests earn trust or make cooperation possible; others can secure a particular faction's practical support. Their immediate human stakes remain meaningful. A completed local favor need not automatically recruit an entire kingdom or resolve its civil war.

Conquest and alliance are both strategic choices. A ruler may secure a neighboring fort by force or gain its use through agreement. Campaign outcomes must account for the resulting troops, access, resources and political costs, rather than treating every border painted in the player's color as equally useful cooperation.

### Proposed implementation of coalition strength

Represent support through specific commitments, such as field forces, food, transport, access, intelligence or safe refuge. These are design examples, not newly assigned benefits for particular existing quests. A treaty should say who promised what and under which conditions. When fulfilled, it should produce the corresponding people, resources or access in the shared world.

Keep those commitments separate from territorial control. Losing a capital need not erase every loyal company, and conquering that capital need not transfer all of its former ruler's allies. Occupation can provide resources while also requiring garrisons and provoking resistance. This allows different combinations of allies and conquered territory to support a viable response.

Show consequences in the existing main map and event history: a faction agrees to passage, sends a force, refuses a summons or loses a supply route. Avoid reducing the entire main quest to an unexplained alliance score.

## Thalmagar and the older peoples

Thalmagar works to align with or gain control over centaurs, elves, dwarves and goblins, forming a coalition of those older peoples. His efforts belong to the active political campaign. The Eshtor undead faction, Goblinland and both Baldro dwarf states already maintain mutual peace against human expansion, as detailed below. The other factions he initially commands, recruits later, coerces or fails to recruit are not yet all assigned. These peace agreements do not themselves establish vassalage to Thalmagar.

Retain the established separate centaur societies, elven realms and dwarf kingdoms. The current friendly relationships around Yunethre and Wood Elfland do not automatically disappear because Thalmagar seeks partners among those peoples. Race, political allegiance and a particular ruler's agreement need separate records.

A proposed diplomatic system would give each approached faction reasons to cooperate, resist or bargain. The hero could expose the Dark Lord's agents, resolve grievances or offer a competing agreement. These possibilities support the alliance-building objective without presuming an entire species makes one unanimous political choice.

## The undead faction and plague

The undead are a distinct race and faction system. The **Blood Plague** is a working name for the fictional affliction: it kills a person and transforms them into a rotting undead being that remains cognizant. This is not limited to conventional mindless zombies, and no fixed vampire rules are established.

Thalmagar creating the undead himself is a proposed origin. Whether he is undead, how his necromancy began, and the relationship between his powers and the plague's original creation remain open. The duke at the Forsaken Citadel is definitely undead and a necromancer; he deliberately works to spread the affliction.

Transmission, timing, prevention, treatment, reversibility, military recruitment and the degree of personal agency after transformation are not yet specified. Neither cognizance nor being undead alone establishes automatic obedience to Thalmagar. Faction allegiance and any magical control must be authored separately.

### Persistent people and explicit reanimation

The existing rule against resetting casualties means mode switching, reloading or rebuilding a scene must not restore a dead person as healthy and living. Necromantic reanimation is an explicit in-world event and an exception to permanent bodily inactivity, not an accidental reset.

The proposed state model retains one person's identity, records their death, and then records transformation to an undead state. Memories, relationships, wounds and body condition can remain attached to that identity. Living and undead copies of the same person must not coexist. Infection, death, raising and destruction are separate saved events; none is inferred merely because the player changes modes.

## Forsaken Citadel on Eshtor Plateau

The **Forsaken Citadel** occupies an ancient site on Eshtor Plateau that had stood abandoned for centuries and was reputed to be haunted. Its settlement is a small enclosed, walled town dominated by a towering, many-storied structure. The architecture should make the tower's extraordinary height the defining landmark. An exact height, floor count, footprint and plateau position remain to be designed.

The resident is the **Duke of North Ganun**, a working title to be expressed in Ganun's language later. He relocated to the abandoned Citadel and claims the plateau for Ganun. North Ganun remains his origin and title; it is not the Citadel's current location and does not become undead territory through this relocation.

| Political layer | Situation |
| --- | --- |
| Public title | Duke of North Ganun, supposedly a vassal of Ganun's lower king |
| Public territorial claim | The duke presents his occupation of Eshtor as a claim for Ganun |
| Effective government | A separate undead faction based at the Forsaken Citadel, outside Ganun's effective control |
| Secret allegiance | The duke is actually a vassal of Thalmagar |
| Personal nature and activity | Undead necromancer deliberately spreading the plague |

The faction is independent of Ganun in practice while secretly subordinate to Thalmagar. That distinction must survive map presentation and diplomatic rules. Ganun's purported claim does not overwrite Eshtor's actual controller. The earlier proposed location immediately below the West Baldro Mountains does not transfer as a geographic constraint to Eshtor.

## Rumors and recognition

The people of North Ganun fear the duke and try to make others recognize the threat. Much of the rest of Ganun is skeptical of their horror stories; similar skepticism extends to Lond, Riesov and the Baldro neighbors. Retain this contrast between frightened witnesses and distant disbelief after relocating the duke. The exact incidents, travel routes and witnesses connecting North Ganun to his new Eshtor seat remain to be written.

His undead nature and true allegiance are not generally understood. The discovery sequence should distinguish suspicion, evidence of necromancy or plague activity, and proof of service to Thalmagar. Those facts can have different diplomatic consequences. A proposed quest sequence could turn local testimony into evidence sufficient to win a hearing, assistance or an alliance, but no particular proof object, victim or outcome is fixed yet.

The entire geographic map remains visible during development, but ordinary diplomatic information does not automatically reveal concealed allegiances. Geographic visibility and political knowledge are separate systems. A developer truth inspector may expose the actual relationships without granting that knowledge to the hero or other rulers.

## Wilhelm and concealed undead infiltration

**Prince Wilhelm, the Blood Prince, is secretly undead and a vassal of Thalmagar.** He can maintain a convincing mortal appearance. His existing pale hair and princely presentation can function as that guise; there are indications of his true nature, but undeath must not be an immediately obvious label in ordinary dialogue or diplomacy. His precise undead subtype remains open.

Wilhelm's reinforcing soldiers are also concealed undead. They can disguise themselves as soldiers or pass as civilians, allowing infiltration into human societies and coups from within. Their appearance should support discovery through hero observation rather than universally obvious rotting-monster silhouettes. Particular tells, disguise mechanics, exposure conditions and numbers remain to be designed. Do not assign this nature to Cedric's original elite guard or every resident of Minora by association.

Minora has two successive takeovers. Cedric's surprise seizure of the League government has already happened at the starting snapshot. Wilhelm and his army have just arrived under the appearance of reinforcing him. The planned development over the first few campaign cycles is an undead coup from inside the city. It is not already completed at the opening and does not wait solely for a hero to visit or for the player to press an end-turn button. Exact timing, the degree to which early intervention can prevent or alter it, Cedric's fate and the resulting territorial reach remain open.

Seeing disguised troops in Minora can give the hero evidence of an undead presence. Exposing that presence, identifying the responsible faction and proving Wilhelm's vassalage to Thalmagar are related but distinct discoveries. Uncovering one does not silently grant every other hidden fact. The hero may discover these developments by visiting in person while the live campaign continues.

## The Blood Prince's island faction

There are **two distinct northern undead factions**: the stronger Eshtor faction and the Blood Prince's less well supported island faction. The latter is Wilhelm's existing Thalmagar vassal establishment, now given territory; it is not a new unrelated vassal or a subordinate of the Eshtor duke.

Use the atlas names **West Ithzel** and **East Ithzel** for the island. Wilhelm formerly controlled the whole island, then departed with his principal army for Minora. The human Lower King has returned and is reclaiming the kingdom from the east. At the opening, the human restoration holds East Ithzel and Wilhelm's remaining undead faction holds West Ithzel. Each seeks to recover the other half; exact settlements, garrisons and local control boundaries remain open.

The latest balance is **roughly 50/50**, so either side can win without the hero. This supersedes any implication of a predetermined winner in the dictated description. The island is an early opportunity for the player to tip the conflict against the undead. Eshtor remains the stronger undead faction because Wilhelm's island establishment is receiving less support while he concentrates on his southern campaign.

An undead victory creates a second advance toward Ganun that can threaten it sooner than the Eshtor expansion. The sea crossing or route, forces and exact pace remain to be authored; do not turn geographic proximity into instant troop movement. A human victory removes or diminishes that foothold according to the actual battle and survivor outcomes.

Once either side wins control of the island, Wilhelm makes his major effort in southern Azhora. He and his army have already arrived at Minora at the initial snapshot. Treat resolution of the island conflict as the newly specified lead-in to that major commitment; its precise relationship to the earlier planned first-cycles coup still needs authoring. The player now **starts in Minora**, rather than Drent, in this intended campaign scenario. The UI prototype reflects that location; the existing game's tutorial and saves are unchanged.

The faction's undead nature and Thalmagar relationship follow the existing discovery rules. Its public presentation can identify Wilhelm's island holdings without automatically proving undeath or exposing the overlord. This does not make every resident or soldier on the island undead.

## Hidden vassals and the diplomatic view

Thalmagar conceals much of his reach through secret vassals. The currently established relationships are:

| Actor | Apparent position at the opening | Actual relationship |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duke at the Forsaken Citadel on Eshtor Plateau | Duke of North Ganun claiming an outlying possession for Ganun | Ruler of a separate undead faction, secretly vassal to Thalmagar |
| Prince Wilhelm, his western Ithzel faction and infiltrating force | Ambroni prince with western island holdings, arriving to support Cedric at Minora | Concealed undead prince and ruler of the second northern undead faction, secretly vassal to Thalmagar |
| North Gorgi Mountains goblin faction | Independent member of the loose Goblinland confederation | Secret vassal of Thalmagar, preparing his major later invasion army |

Eshtor Plateau, the Forsaken Citadel and its undead duke describe one territorial vassal establishment, not three separate vassals. Wilhelm and North Gorgi are two other vassals. No direct hierarchy placing either beneath the duke is established. The other three goblin factions are not automatically Thalmagar's vassals. Additional hidden vassals may be added later.

The ordinary faction diplomatic view initially shows apparent relationships and known information. It must not expose Thalmagar's secret vassal edges merely because their actors or territory appear on the full map. Hero exploration and investigation are the principal way to uncover the true faction and its hidden superior; discovering those ties without personal investigation is intended to be difficult. Other intelligence methods are not yet specified.

The proposed information model stores actual allegiance, presented allegiance, the hero's evidence and what other rulers accept separately. Evidence can be retained through saves and mode changes. Publicly convincing a skeptical ruler can require more than the hero privately recognizing an undead soldier. Automatic alliances, a ruler's belief or worldwide diplomatic exposure must not follow from a bare map click.

## Acor Wetlands dispute

**Acor Wetlands** is disputed between **Acreland**, the existing Acorwood faction, the **Lower Kingdom of Endevor**, and **Thalmagar's Empire**. The user confirmed that the dictated Vandor means Endevor. This is a three-way dispute, with no sole controller or precise occupied holdings yet assigned.

All three claims belong on the campaign map; contested status does not require painting the whole region as permanently owned by any one claimant. Acorwood, Endevor and Thalmagar's Narcosh have actual atlas approaches to the wetlands. The dispute does not make Acreland or Endevor a Thalmagar vassal.

## Goblinland: independent allies and the North Gorgi muster

**Goblinland is a loose confederation of four independent allied factions**, not one country with a single ruler or shared army. Orgmala, South Gorgi, Gorgiwood and North Gorgi each retain their own government, forces and decisions. They cooperate little despite their alliance. A confederation label can group them in the campaign view, but selecting it must still reveal the separate members and their actual holdings.

All four want to invade **Lond**, the richest and strongest nearby human power confronting them. For much of the game their normal pressure consists of periodic raids rather than a successful combined conquest. This regional comparison coexists with the earlier ruling that Ambron is stronger than Lond overall. Raid targets, intervals, armies and outcomes remain to be authored; the members do not automatically synchronize their raids.

**North Gorgi alone among these four is presently established as a secret Thalmagar vassal.** It spends a substantial part of the campaign building a much stronger goblin/orc army intended for the middle-to-late offensive. The user's goblin/orc description establishes its military concept; exact unit types, species distinctions, troop counts and strength are still open. This army is a specific prepared force, not every goblin faction's forces pooled by default.

Its buildup takes campaign time. The proposed model tracks preparation, available troops and supplies, so interference can have visible consequences. The intended scenario gives the army a readiness event after its long muster; exact duration, effects of disruption and conditions for delaying or preventing its release are still to be designed. Finishing preparation does not retroactively conquer its march route.

## Northern expansion and regional peace

The Eshtor undead lord, all four Goblinland members and the West and East Baldro dwarf states are mutually at peace. Their common interest is resisting humans whom they see as threatening to overtake their lands. This gives the duke a protected political neighborhood and constrains his expansion: he does not plan to conquer those goblin and dwarf partners to his south and west or elsewhere along their borders. Peace is distinct from shared government, military access, a joint army or secret vassalage. The separate explicit allegiance of North Gorgi to Thalmagar does not extend that allegiance to its confederates or the dwarves.

The duke instead favors slow expansion against vulnerable human kingdoms. **Witherst and Riesov** are the first major targets; **East Ganun and then the rest of Ganun** are the next intended direction. **Thoth** is also open to pressure on a separate branch. The advance takes a long time, with local conquest and the spread of undeath progressively removing human communities if outside intervention does not stop it. This sustained regional threat is separate from Thalmagar's later concentrated invasion.

The existing atlas supports the following candidate land connections. They establish adjacent regions, not yet usable army routes; passes, river crossings, supply and access still need checking when orders are authored.

| Branch | Adjacent atlas regions | Design meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Riesov and Ganun | Eshtor Plateau → optionally East Witherst → North Riesov → South Riesov → East Ganun → North, West or South Ganun | Pressure can move through the first threatened kingdoms toward Ganun; no kingdom's fall is predetermined |
| Thoth | Eshtor Plateau → West Witherst → North Thoth → Central Thoth → South Thoth | An additional human frontier, not a direct march from Ganun, which does not border Thoth |

Existing peace with Goblinland does not automatically allow marching through its regions. A route through North Gorgi toward Thoth would require separately established access. The duke's preferences guide faction decisions while local defenses, supplies, player action and other rulers can change what actually happens.

Northern quests are time-sensitive consequences of that advance. The hero can help threatened people before their communities fall and their inhabitants become hostile undead. Those transformations can permanently remove the original quest options and turn familiar people into enemies. Conquest alone does not instantly transform everyone, and recapturing a place does not automatically cure its people or restore their quests. Specific rescue or reclamation follow-ups require their own authored survivors and conditions.

## From remote expansion to the southern invasion

Thalmagar initially advances through the Eshtor undead faction in remote, isolated northern lands. Their distance helps conceal the scale and source of the danger. If left entirely unchecked, this campaign can overrun all of the northern human kingdoms. That is an intended possible outcome of prolonged neglect, not an ownership change that automatically happens on a fixed date.

His preferred preparation is to have the undead control much of the north by the time North Gorgi's army is ready. The two vassals then serve complementary roles: the undead secure territory, while the prepared goblin/orc army supplies the major southward blow. In that successful enemy scenario, the army moves through friendly territory and authorized routes with little opposition before reaching the surviving southern powers. Safe passage depends on actual control and access. Human survivors, defended crossings, remaining hostile holdings and player interventions can make the same march contested.

The user's confirmed northern corridor is **North Lond → West Lond → East Endevor → South Endevor → Acor Wetlands**. Reaching North Lond from North Gorgi requires an intervening region such as Gorgiwood or South Gorgi. Their confederation alliance is not itself a blanket passage rule; access needs an agreement. The duke's first eastward gains in Riesov and Ganun do not automatically secure Lond or Endevor: later operations must actually open this western and southern corridor, including overcoming strong Lond. Acor Wetlands begins disputed between Acreland, Endevor and Thalmagar and does not become safe merely because the invasion is scheduled.

The southern objectives are **Mithala and Celder**, followed by a main drive toward **Yunethre**, **Isareos and Minora**, and ultimately **Ambron**. Smaller columns secure mountain approaches while the principal army continues toward the major settlements. The exact mountains, passes and fork points remain to be selected. These are military objectives, not a rule that every inhabitant or existing faction in those regions is human or hostile to Thalmagar.

Minora's role depends on the earlier political outcome. If Wilhelm still holds it for Thalmagar, the arriving army can reinforce or rendezvous at an allied stronghold. If resistance controls it, the same objective requires a siege, battle or negotiated entry. The scenario must read the saved coup and subsequent control changes rather than force a second hostile conquest of an already allied city.

The user confirmed "Northland/Westland" as North Lond/West Lond, "Nethri" as Yunethre and "Sarios" as Isareos; "Hitasarios/Hirasarious" is treated as another mention of Isareos, not a separate objective. "Menoria" follows the established Minora name and "Brawn/Bron" follows Ambron. The final approach from Isareos toward Elagos and Ambron passes through intervening territory such as Meneth. Exact holding-level routes still need to account for actual crossings and defenses.

Throughout the offensive, smaller forces keep raiding across different regions while the main army advances. As a proposed simulation rule, create these forces through recorded recruitment, reinforcement or detachment from an existing army. Record their source, allegiance, travel and losses so entering hero mode finds the same troops and defeating a raiding group matters. The exact replacement budget and military economy are open; repeated mode changes must not manufacture fresh raiders.

Ambron is intended to be the hardest major objective in this southern drive. Its fall should put the resistance close to defeat, but no automatic game-over rule is established. A prepared player should be able to repel the invasion and then undertake a difficult counteroffensive, potentially against an enemy that already controls much of the world. Recovery, final liberation objectives and victory conditions still need design.

The main strategic purpose of early and middle play is to limit Thalmagar's subordinate factions before this crisis matures. Northern relief can preserve future allies and interrupt the safe invasion corridor. Southern politics and Wilhelm's concealed power demand attention at the same time. His coup is planned early, while a middle-game revelation of his true allegiance is an intended story beat; hero investigation may discover it earlier. A reveal changes knowledge and political reaction, not the earlier date on which he actually became a vassal.

## Crisis behavior to develop

The Eshtor undead lord expands against **Witherst and Riesov** early in the campaign, making them his first major human targets. Northern silver-grade quests become available gradually to help people resist that pressure and obtain intervention from more distant human kingdoms. Initial quest information should concern local danger and the duke's activities; it does not immediately name Thalmagar as the overarching enemy. See the [northern silver quest design](northern-silver-quests-design.md) for the proposed relief, defense, investigation and mobilization sequence.

Use preparation and visible world events to make escalation understandable. Proposed influences include mustered forces, supply, recruited partners, fortified routes and opposition to the duke. Player successes should be able to weaken or delay offensives and save particular places. Exact thresholds, setbacks, recovery behavior and the final victory condition remain open; the overarching enemy does not imply every local defeat is scripted or every player success must be undone.

The plague and Citadel can foreshadow the greater crisis and connect small-scale hero work to campaign diplomacy. Defeating the duke would affect his faction and Thalmagar's support, but is not yet defined as automatically ending the plague or defeating Thalmagar. Likewise, winning the Ambroni succession war does not by itself complete the overarching main quest.

## After Thalmagar's defeat

The main crisis can conclude while the same world keeps running. Surviving factions still have interests, rivalries, damaged lands and relationships formed during the war. Former enemies, displaced people and transformed inhabitants do not vanish because the main quest is complete. Defeating Thalmagar does not automatically cure the undead, restore earlier borders, erase independent factions or force permanent peace.

The user wants this aftermath to support potentially indefinite observation and further play, with new geopolitical and personal stories. Continuing faction decisions, recovery, succession and evolving institutions are proposed sources of that development. Do not simply restart Thalmagar's crisis at intervals to keep the simulation busy. The [living-world continuation design](living-world-continuation-design.md) records this speculative goal, observer and chronicle ideas, execution choices and long-horizon validation needs. Defining the main quest's victory is therefore separate from defining whether the world has to stop.
