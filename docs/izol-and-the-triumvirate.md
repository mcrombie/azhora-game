# Izol, and the three generals

The user's own history of the Republic, set down so the game and every agent build from one text,
as `the-war-and-the-house-of-ambron.md` does for the Empire. The present year is **980**. Anything
here that contradicts an older note wins. Where this touches the world-builder lore
(`../world-builder/azhora_lore`), the lore's geography and theology are taken as given:
`geography/regions/izol.md` (the island, the goddess, the Assembly at the Hearthstone, **no
capital**), `geography/regions/selemis.md` (the crescent harbour, the outpost network, the first
maritime power of the Iberos Sea).

## What Izol did while Ambron burned

- **978** — While the Empire is tearing itself apart, the Izoli Republic goes to war with
  **Selemis** and **breaks the Selemi fleet at sea**. Nobody expected this. Selemis has been the
  most consequential point of maritime power on the continent for two centuries, and Izol is a
  rocky island with a confederation that has never had an army.
- **979** — Izol **takes Selemis itself**: the crescent harbour, the city, the island. Taking the
  Selemi home port is the great upset of the age. It does not deliver the Selemi empire, because
  that empire was never territory — it was the **outpost network**, and the outposts are not
  cooperating with an Izoli occupation. Selemis wants the Izoli out and says so in every port.
- **980, the game's year** — Izol joins **Prince Ruzo** and the other allies to raise the Coalition
  against the Empire: the Izoli Republic, Suval, the Ambroni rebels, Pyros, Selemis (as a conquered
  subject, which is exactly the awkwardness it sounds like), Marosh and the island cities. The
  Coalition takes Solis.

## The triumvirate

Three generals hold Izol between them. They cooperate, they compete, and everyone on the island
expects them to fight each other eventually. This is the Republic's version of the Empire's
succession: **on the Coalition branch of Chapter 3 the traveler picks one of the three and secures
the country for them**, exactly as the Empire branch picks among Willard, Valroy and Wilhelm.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **General Orsen Kellveth** *(name invented)* | Holds **Solis**. Town-born, out of the coastal commercial towns, the political one: he argues, he bargains, he keeps his agreements past the point where breaking them would pay, which is the Izoli reputation and which he uses. **Where he is depends on the player.** Take the Coalition's side and help it win the border battle, and Solis is his — held by Izol, not by West Izol, and not by the Svaleen Confederation that used to own it. Take the Empire's side and help it win, and Solis is lost: Kellveth has fled home to West Izol, and arrives in Chapter 3 as a general without a command and with everything to prove. |
| **General Tavren Doreth** *(name invented)* | Occupies **Selemis**. He holds the single most strategic island in the Iberos Sea and cannot leave it, because the moment he does the Selemi take it back. He commands the sea and is a prisoner of it. The Selemi colonies and outposts are hostile, the city is sullen, and his soldiers have been garrisoning a rich foreign town for a year, which does to an army what it always does. |
| **General Hesk Marech** *(name invented)* | Besieges **Nylon**. **The best army of the three**, and he knows it. Nylon is held by Prince Wilhelm's forces, who are themselves hostile occupiers, so **the people of Nylon are on the besiegers' side** — the strangest siege in the war, where the city would open its gates if the garrison would let it. Marech is the highland-tribe general: territorial claim, oath, and the least patience for the coastal towns' arithmetic. |

## Why this is dangerous, in Izoli terms

The lore is emphatic that the confederation has **no capital, no enforcement mechanism and no
army**. Its decisions bind because they are sworn at the Hearthstone, before Izol, where all three
of her mountain presences can witness what is said. Three generals with armies abroad — in Solis,
on Selemis, before Nylon — are the first thing in the island's history that could enforce
something. None of them has broken an oath yet. Every Izoli can see what happens if one does:
whoever steps outside the goddess's protection steps outside the bond that holds, and the bond that
holds is the whole of what Izol is.

Write the island to that. Ordinary Izoli are not neutral spectators of the triumvirate — they are
frightened of it, and the fear is religious as much as political.

## What this means for what is built

1. **Chapter 2's outcome must set who holds Solis.** The game already ends Chapter 2 at Solis on the
   Coalition branch and on the Moros Plain on the Empire branch. That flag decides whether Kellveth
   is in Solis or a refugee in West Izol, and both readings have to exist in Chapter 3's content.
2. **Solis, once taken, is held by Izol.** Not by "West Izol", not by the Svaleen Confederation whose
   city it is. Solis was stormed and burned by Wilhelm in 977 and is now garrisoned by an island
   republic that its own citizens did not invite. That is a second scar on a city that already has
   one (see `the-war-and-the-house-of-ambron.md`, "Solis must show its scars").
3. **Selemis is a Coalition member and an occupied country at the same time.** Any Selemi in the
   game — and they are the most travelled people on the continent, so they turn up everywhere —
   knows this and has a view.
4. **The Republic branch of Chapter 3 is a choice among three generals**, matching the Empire
   branch's choice among three princes. West Izol is where that choice is put to the traveler.

## Still open

- Where was the sea battle of 978 fought, and what is it called?
- The three generals' names are invented in the island's register and want the user's approval.
- How did a confederation with no army raise three of them in two years — levies sworn at the
  Hearthstone, town contingents, hired Selemi hulls? The lore leaves this open and it is worth
  answering before West Izol's content is written.
