# Yunethre environment and settlement foundation

Yunethre retains its 27 authored plains hexes between the South Oremindi and West Lotharn Mountains. Region ID 38 is appended to the desktop registry. Registration preserves the former unbuilt ground contribution to neighbouring terrain; the new low grassland relief and settlement grading act only on Yunethre's own cells.

Laketown stands immediately east of the existing Forest Tarn. Its opening position is independent of the Ambroni Empire, Celder and Elfland; Chapter 2 owns subsequent agreements. Seventeen village buildings combine human timber and lime, elven branching eaves, dwarven stonework and slate gables, broad centaur courts, and satyr turf roofs and carved porches. The cultures share the same lanes and unwalled market common. The shoreline and 35-metre lake surface remain owned by South Oremindi. The stone stair and timber pier use physical ramp/deck surfaces to reach the high lake without changing its elevation.

Councillor Rowan is the Chapter 2 council representative: a youthful man with green eyes, copper curls and a long red beard. Ten additional named residents include humans, elves, dwarves, centaurs and satyrs, each with a livelihood and ordinary F conversations. Their short walks stop for visitors, avoid collision, and are built incrementally on arrival. The existing eastern camp remains a separate community; its defeat does not remove Laketown's centaur residents.

The high-shore fishing hut, supported work apron, net racks, creels, sorting table and smokehouse establish the fishing livelihood. One working skiff repeatedly rows out, fishes, returns and unloads; another stays moored. The 82-second local animation follows surveyed water points, pauses when given zero simulation time and uses no campaign harvest commands. The people, fish and houses are presentation, not population or food totals. Any future productive capacity still belongs to campaign.js.

Implementation: `laketown-residents.js` owns the cast and dialogue; `laketown-characters.js` owns the rigs; `laketown-people.js` and `laketown-fishing.js` own local presentation. `yunethre-life-host.js` loads them only after visiting the region. The shared `laketown-waterfront-layout.js` keeps the moving skiff and physical dock aligned; `laketown-waterfront.js` builds the static work area. Chapter 2 retains ownership of Rowan's quest dialogue and progress.

Bane's Camp stands on the eastern half of the passage, separate from the neutral town. A council shade and six felt households have broad entrances, woven cloth bands and clear approaches sized for centaur bodies. A common cooking hearth, raised kitchen benches and provisions, hide-curing frames, a courier's worktable, wheel repairs, three loaded supply carts, bow racks and straw targets make this a working settlement. Its open centre and western exit remain clear. Wagons are supply trailers; there are no horse pens or invented livestock. The tested raiding approach winds south through open Yunethre ground into northwestern Isareos; it avoids both the neutral town and Elfland and follows dry, manageable ground around the existing beck banks.

The Reed Kin are this camp's fictional community, not a universal centaur culture. Six named people replace the three anonymous camp figures: Saera remembers witnessed promises; Varek hunts and reads tracks; Delan repairs hides and carrying gear; Arven tends the common meal and stores; Ilyra repairs carts; Kesh carries messages and learns routes. Each has an individual coat, craft clothing and hand tool, a short working route, and F conversations about livelihood, obligations, war and disagreements. These residents pause with the game, stop to speak, avoid obstacles and visitors, and leave the visible cast when the chapter disables the camp. Laketown's independent centaur residents remain.

Kinship includes adopted family and accepted responsibilities. Honour is expressed through keeping a limited, witnessed promise, feeding guests, sharing the hunt and showing restraint. The conversations also expose failings: preference for relatives, elders silencing younger voices, craft work denied a voice in council, and grief used to justify retaliation. Their political views differ; they are not all fighters. Peace-specific lines require the actual Chapter 2 state and never infer an agreement from Chapter 1's history. The ordinary dialogue cannot make treaties or award quest progress.

Bane has three optional personal conversations alongside his negotiations. He recalls his work as a salt-and-wool carter, his obligations to the households, and a retaliatory grain raid that harmed an innocent driver. His wish to protect the camp competes with pride and his reluctance to give the craftspeople a council voice. These topics return to negotiation without advancing time or recording consent.

The design uses broad references to mobile households, exchange, practical craft and reciprocity, without copying sacred designs or treating distinct real peoples as interchangeable. Reference reading includes the Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian's [A Song for the Horse Nation](https://americanindian.si.edu/exhibitions/horsenation/) and the Metropolitan Museum of Art's [Scythian Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/scythian-art). The Reed Kin, their names, witness cords and personal disputes are new fiction for Azhora.

`centaur-culture.js` owns this cast and its words; `centaur-camp-layout.js` supplies the shared work locations; `centaur-camp-characters.js` adds clothes and animated tools to the existing noble beastfolk rig; `centaur-camp-people.js` handles local routines and targeting. `yunethre-life.js` combines them with Laketown and wildlife. Camp presentation adds no food output, population counts or simulation orders. Focused validation is in `tests/centaur-camp.test.js`, with shared dialogue and life checks in `tests/resident-host.test.js` and `tests/yunethre-life.test.js`.

The centaur–Celder encounter takes place in the natural meadow immediately south of the camp, with low boundary stones and two small pennants outside its fighting area. After fifteen active minutes of Chapter 2, the battlefield marker leads to an F interaction with an explicit choice: defend the centaurs, fight for Celder, or remain neutral. After setting out, **F8 → Chapter 2 / Advance to the battle opening** reaches that same choice sooner. Agreeing the safe-passage compact with both parties before the deadline averts the encounter entirely. Entering its briefing does not commit a side; starting the fight does.

The current local encounter has four centaur archers, three Celder knights and Teresod; it does not instantiate whole campaign armies. Centaur archers keep their distance and use bows, while Celder lancers announce and commit to straight charges. Teresod can fight on foot or horseback. Hold **R** to draw the bow and release to shoot; **click / F** strikes with the staff; **Space** gives an on-foot dodge or mounted surge; **G** mounts or dismounts when stopped; **Tab** changes target; **WASD / arrows** move, **Shift** runs or canters, and **Escape** pauses for tactics. A mounted surge gives speed, not invulnerability. Right-drag turns the camera. The joined encounter holds the chapter clock and ends when a formation breaks, Teresod falls or withdraws, or the three-minute field limit decides the remaining strength. Its result stays visible for review before returning to exploration.

`gameplay/combat/yunethre-cavalry.js` owns physical encounter rules; the chapter's `cavalry-view.js` presents them in the existing world. Political consequences remain under `simulation/campaign.js` through Chapter 2's authored rules: the host submits the chosen side and one actual result. Winning this field does not annex Laketown or grant Celder an alliance. Rowan's aftermath conversation still matters, and the separate conquest of Laketown is not yet playable. For isolated combat testing, F8 offers **Cavalry rehearsal / Defend the centaurs** and **Computer rehearsal / Centaurs versus Celder**. These temporary encounters do not submit chapter decisions, block saving during the exercise, and return to the prior exploration state. The computer uses the same movement, attacks, ammunition and damage rules as a player. Focused checks live in `tests/yunethre-cavalry.test.js` and `tests/yunethre-cavalry-host.test.js`; `src/dev/checks/yunethre-cavalry-smoke.js` exercises the real camp, dialogue, controls and campaign connection.

The chosen side matters after the fight: a centaur victory with Teresod's support records their protection and permits the council to consider accession, subject to elven relations. Fighting for Celder closes peaceful accession even if its army loses. For that route, return to Rowan and accept Laketown's independence to continue; the unavailable conquest is not required to finish this playable chapter. Observing a Celder victory without joining preserves the council's voluntary accession option.

Low shoulders, tawny grass, scattered flowers, stone fragments and irregular sheltered groves provide variety while retaining broad open plains. Every visible tree has a registered harvestable species: white oak, silver birch or hawthorn. Own random streams and spatial instance batches preserve other regions' scenery and avoid one draw call per plant.

Twenty-five permanent non-settlement habitats support 50 deer, boar and upland hares. The town and camp cells remain clear. Wildlife uses the existing resident controller, retaining identities, testing body clearance and slope, and staying within its region when roaming or fleeing. Culling never recreates the population.

Trade simulation, camp permissions and the wider centaur campaign remain separate design work. The political premise is survival after human expansion, with Elfland trading with and supporting the centaurs. The new civilian cast adds local conversations without rewriting Chapter 1 history or Chapter 2 diplomacy.

For native regression checks, run `node scripts/launch.cjs --exploration --yunethre --smoke-test --internal-review --yunethre-cavalry-checks`. This covers camp conversations, the paused briefing, real controls, computer combat, result review, returning with the owned horse, and reporting a live outcome to the campaign. `--yunethre-camp-checks` instead captures the camp, Saera's cultural conversation and Bane's personal history. Both use an isolated in-memory save; images and reports go to `tests/artifacts/`.

Validation: all eight focused Yunethre checks passed, including actual creation of 50 resident animals, grounded movement for every represented species, culling and revisiting, typed trees, the dry raiding circuit and connected promenade support. The four-check combined-world integration pass also passed: main-road ordering, settlement entrances, Minora bridges, Caricas routes and accessible farm benches, and Yunethre tracks, centaur clearance and the entire lakeside ramp. That integration run preceded the final Caricas farm relocations; it does not claim to validate those later coordinates.
